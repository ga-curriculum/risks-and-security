The System Under Audit

Meet ARIA

ARIA is an Autonomous Research and Insights Agent deployed by Nexus Analytics. ARIA has access to three external tools and runs on an automated schedule. You've been brought in after an incident report was filed.

System Architecture — ARIA v2.1
🔍 Web Search Tool
Queries any public URL. Returns full page content including HTML. No content filtering applied.
📧 Email Tool
Read from and send to any company email. Runs under the CEO's identity. No send confirmation required.
🗄️ CRM Database
Full read/write access to 4,200 customer records including PII and contracts. No field-level access control.

ARIA's stated purpose: Generate weekly competitive intelligence reports by researching competitors online, summarizing findings, and emailing the report to the sales team.

What triggered the audit — 3 incidents in 30 days: (1) A competitor's website appeared to crash during ARIA's research session. (2) A sales rep received an email from "the CEO" asking for customer data she didn't recognize sending. (3) A customer complained about receiving a marketing email they never opted into.

🚨 Why agents are different from traditional software: Traditional software does exactly what its code says. Agents interpret natural-language instructions and can be manipulated through the content they process — not just through code exploits. A competitor's website, a contact form, or an email body can all become attack vectors if the agent treats their content as trusted instructions.
Section 01 · Analysis

Threat Classification

Below are 10 behaviors observed in ARIA's logs. Classify each as SAFE or RISK. You'll see the risk category and explanation after each choice.

💡 Ask for each behavior: Could this action — without additional validation — cause unintended harm, leak sensitive data, or be hijacked by an external actor? If yes: Risk. Regardless of intent.
Section 02 · Live Simulation

Exploit Simulation

Two of the three incidents were caused by known attack patterns. Watch each execute in the terminal — see what ARIA processed, what decision it made, and what the consequence was.

⚠️ These are real attack categories. Prompt injection and data exfiltration via email are responsible for the majority of known AI agent security incidents in production deployments.
Exploit 1 of 2 — Prompt Injection via Web Search

The Competitor's Hidden Payload

Exploit 2 of 2 — Data Exfiltration via Email Tool

The Poisoned Contact Form

Section 03 · Design Activity

Guardrail Design

Five risks identified. For each one, select the guardrail that addresses the root cause — not just the symptom. Then see the decision flow it creates in ARIA's architecture.

💡 Guardrail design principle: A guardrail should be the minimum intervention that prevents the harm. Over-constraining makes the agent useless. Under-constraining makes it dangerous. The right guardrail blocks the attack vector without blocking the intended behavior.
Section 04 · Ethics Evaluation

Ethical Implications

Security risks and ethical risks are related but distinct. A fully secured agent can still harm users through normal operation — through discriminatory scoring, consent violations, or removing human judgment from consequential decisions. Evaluate three ARIA scenarios.

🔮 The distinction: A security risk is exploitable by an external attacker. An ethical risk is baked into the agent's design — it harms people through normal operation, not through attack. Both require mitigation. Neither excuses the other.
Section 05 · Deliverable

Security Audit Report

Assemble the formal audit report for ARIA. This is the document the security team, legal counsel, and engineering leads would use to prioritize remediation. Specificity matters — vague reports produce vague fixes.

💡 Good audit reports name the attack vector, the specific tool involved, the data at risk, the guardrail addressing it at the root, and the ethical implication. They don't describe problems without proposing solutions.
